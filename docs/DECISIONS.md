# Decisions

Key decisions, why we made them, and what they cost. A new decision is added here in the same commit as the work it affects. The agreed problem space is in [PROBLEM_SPACE.md](PROBLEM_SPACE.md).

Status is **Accepted** (agreed with the user), **Proposed** (awaiting the user), or **Superseded** (replaced; the entry stays and points to its replacement).

## Decision log

| # | Decision | Why | Trade-off | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Split per item = COGS + provider margin + 75 bps fee. No platform markup. | PRD requirement 3; the PRD's metric is "the 75 bps we earn". | The fee is our only revenue, and real card fees are larger than it. | Accepted |
| D2 | The fee is 0.75% of the full item price, built into the price, and never shown to the patient as its own line. | The patient pays exactly the price the provider set (requirement 1). COGS is fixed and brands can't be charged. A visible fee on a clinician's recommendation costs trust for 27¢. | The provider's share absorbs the fee unless they raise the price. | Accepted |
| D3 | No-profit means a $0 margin at the lowest price (COGS $20 → $20.16, with the fee rounded up per D17). The platform keeps nothing extra. | The PRD has no platform markup. It beats Fullscript's $26 (full discount) and $36 (no-profit default). | We give up a Fullscript-style spread. | Accepted |
| D4 | Integer cents. The fee is rounded once per item, and the margin is computed by subtraction. | Every cent is traceable and the parts always add up. | The order's fee can differ from 0.75% × order total by up to ½¢ per item. | Superseded by D17 |
| D5 | The provider can enter a price or a margin. The margin is recomputed from the final price. | Requirement 1 allows either, and the fee depends on the price. | The margin can be 1¢ off what the provider typed. | Accepted |
| D6 | The patient pays for the exact order and can't add items. | Browsing is out of scope, and requirement 1 has the provider assemble the order. | No patient self-service. | Accepted |
| D7 | Providers keep an item list drawn from a shared seeded catalog. No stock tracking. | The user's reading of requirement 5 ("update inventory" means which items the provider sells). | We can't stop someone selling an item we're out of. | Accepted |
| D8 | Every paid order placed with us counts as volume moved off third-party sites. | The PRD says the volume already exists. | It also counts orders that are new, not moved (see Known limitations in PROBLEM_SPACE.md). | Accepted |
| D9 | Shipping, tax, real card fees, and refunds are out of the slice. | The PRD puts them out of scope. | The real economics aren't modelled. | Accepted |
| D10 | The provider can change an item's price on any order. It starts at their usual price. | Changing the usual price for one patient and then changing it back adds friction for our main user. | Prices can differ between patients of the same provider. | Accepted |
| D11 | The product is a provider portal (My store, New order, Sales, Order details) plus one patient checkout per order, presented as the provider's store. | Requirement 1 has the provider assemble the order, and this ties each payment to a patient. A shared store link couldn't do that, and anyone holding it could buy. | The patient can't add items. | Accepted |
| D12 | We polish the UX, including product images, beyond what the PRD asks, but only on New order and Checkout. | The user is a front-end engineer and wants a good experience. The graders don't score visual design, and we have 1–2 days. | Time spent on UI isn't spent elsewhere. | Accepted |
| D13 | The margin is paid to the provider. Each provider belongs to a practice. | The PRD says to "route the provider's margin to the provider". | Payouts to a practice aren't supported yet. | Accepted |
| D14 | Prices are capped at MSRP. | It protects patients, and prices across providers can only vary downward. | Providers can't charge above retail. | Accepted |
| D15 | The demo uses real brand and product names with real retail prices, our own illustrated images, a "Demo — not a real store" notice, and is hidden from search engines. | Real names make it feel realistic. Brand photos carry copyright risk on a public URL and repo; the worst realistic outcome is a takedown request. | The images don't match the real packaging. | Accepted |
| D16 | Each item in My store has a saved usual price that pre-fills new orders. | It saves the provider clicks. | One more field per store item. | Accepted |
| D17 | Integer cents. The fee is 0.75% of the item price rounded **up** to the next whole cent, once per item. The margin is computed by subtraction. Replaces D4. | D4 didn't say which way to round. The user chose to round in the platform's favour, so any fraction of a cent goes to the fee. One rule with no tie case is simple to test. | The provider's margin can be up to 1¢ lower per item than with ordinary rounding. The lowest price for a $20.00 cost is $20.16, not $20.15. The order's fee can exceed 0.75% × order total by just under 1¢ per item. | Accepted |
| D18 | Build and load-test for the market leader's volume: about 20k orders a day, ~3 a second at the busiest hour. The paths to every practitioner-channel patient (~30/s) and every US supplement user (~800/s) are written down, not built. k6 load tests at all three levels, plus a same-order race test and a database outage drill, run on the user's laptop and go in a report. | Sized from research, not guessed (ARCHITECTURE.md §2). Even the theoretical ceiling averages ~77 orders a second. The PRD doesn't grade scale, but the user wants to show it was thought through. | Time spent on load tests isn't spent on features. A laptop run measures the laptop at Level 3. | Accepted |
| D19 | Backups only, no standby database. The design fails safely: Pay can't charge without first saving the attempt. | A standby costs money and gives a demo nothing. Losing a paid order is the real risk, and the save-first rule plus backups cover it. | If the database goes down, the site is down until it recovers. | Accepted |
| D20 | One Next.js app server, split into modules (Access, Store, Orders, Payments, Reporting, Pricing, Ports), plus one PostgreSQL database. | Paying, the order's status, and the audit entry change in one database transaction. Separate services would turn that into a two-system consistency problem. Fullscript runs this market on one Rails application. | Every module deploys together. | Accepted |
| D21 | Pay saves a pending attempt **before** charging. A sweep asks the payment company about attempts left pending and settles them. It never charges again. | Any failure leaves a record we can find. Charging first could take money with no record, and Sam would pay twice. | A short window where an attempt is pending. A background sweep is needed. | Accepted |
| D22 | At most one pending or succeeded attempt per order (a partial unique index). The order row is locked for Pay, Cancel order and New link. Success is recorded with conditional updates. A unique Pay key per request. | Stops double charges from double clicks, two tabs, and the sweep racing a slow request, and still holds if the code has a bug. | Requests for the same order wait their turn, which costs a few milliseconds. | Accepted |
| D23 | An unexplained Pay failure shows "We're confirming your payment", never "declined". | Wrongly showing "confirming" fixes itself within seconds. Wrongly showing "declined" can lead Sam to pay twice or dispute a real charge. | Sam sometimes waits a few seconds without needing to. | Accepted |
| D24 | The confirming page gets updates by SSE (the user's idea). Status changes are announced through Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY. | One-way server-to-browser updates, built into browsers, reconnecting on their own. NOTIFY reaches the right connection on any server copy, and avoids memory sharing problems inside Next.js. | Needs a server that stays running and a direct database connection. | Accepted |
| D25 | Pay links are signed: an HMAC of the order ref and link version. They expire 30 days after Send. New link raises the version. Every failed link shows the same "isn't valid" page. | The user's intuition: the same order gives the same link. Copy link always works, a double-clicked Send returns one link, and the database holds no secret. One message for failed links reveals nothing and still points Sam to the fix. | Changing the signing key turns off every live link. | Accepted |
| D26 | Order lifecycle: draft (saves itself) → sent → paid; sent → needs review → paid or sent; Discard draft or Cancel order → cancelled. Expired and "payment in progress" are worked out, not stored. Cancel order and New link wait while a payment is in progress. | No lost work when a provider is pulled into a visit. Drafts give the "time to send" metric a start time. A cancel can't race a payment. | Two staff editing one draft: the last save wins. | Accepted |
| D27 | Each line's split, cost, retail price, name and image freeze at Send. Paying computes nothing. | Catalog and fee-rate changes never alter a sent order, and Sam pays exactly what Dr. Rivera saw. | A draft uses live prices, so Send may flag a line that moved out of range. | Accepted |
| D28 | Quantity of 1–10 per line. The split is worked out per bottle, then multiplied. | Providers can order two bottles. Per-bottle rounding follows D17's "once per item". The cap stops typos. | Two bottles at $36.10 earn $31.64 rather than $31.65. | Accepted |
| D29 | Sales totals use the practice's time zone. Platform-wide totals use UTC. | A clinic's "October" should match its own calendar. UTC is one zone with no daylight-saving jumps. | Per-practice months don't add up exactly to platform months. | Accepted |
| D30 | No patient name on the pay page, and no patient login or verification in the slice. | The PRD puts auth out of scope. A forwarded link shouldn't reveal who the patient is. A login step adds friction for older, unwell patients. | Whoever holds a link can see and pay for that order. Next step: a one-time code by SMS. | Accepted |
| D31 | One Sales list of every order, each with its status's action (including Order again), search by patient or ref, and filters for status and a created, sent or paid date range. Headline totals cover this month by paid date and ignore filters. A footer row adds up the paid orders in view. | One list is simpler. Order again covers repeat orders cheaply, and `source_order_id` makes repeats countable. Split totals answer both "how's my month?" and "what does this view add up to?" | One extra row of totals. | Accepted |
| D32 | Fake login issues a JWT in an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie. Only HS256 is accepted. | Scripts can't read it, it's sent on page loads and SSE, and it's the same shape real login will use. | One session can't be cut off before it expires. | Accepted |
| D33 | TypeScript end to end in Next.js 16 (self-hosted); PostgreSQL with Drizzle; React Aria, Zod, jose, pino; Vitest with fast-check, Playwright with axe, and k6. | Six reasons for TypeScript: shared money code, typed contracts, one deployable, a workload that fits Node, one toolchain, and the user's strongest language. Postgres has every database feature the design uses. Next.js because the user knows it. | Floating-point numbers, handled by integer cents only. The sweep starts from `instrumentation.ts`, which isn't its documented purpose. | Accepted |
| D34 | $0 hosting: Render Free for the app, Neon Free for Postgres. Upgrade the app to Render Starter ($7/mo) only if the wake-up delay hurts grading. | It's a demo. Free tiers cover it once the sweep also runs at server start and the seed can rebuild the data. | The first visit after 15 idle minutes takes about a minute, and the database restores back only 6 hours. Production would cost about $13.30/mo. | Accepted |
| D35 | Palette A (US Web Design System blue), Source Sans 3 for the portal, and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next at 19px for patients. WCAG 2.2 AA, plus support for contrast themes and increased-contrast settings. | The user preferred A on sight. A colour-blindness check found A's primary about twice as distinct from success green as C's teal. Contrast passes everywhere. | Reads somewhat like a government service. No dark mode yet. | Accepted |
| D36 | Check layers: `npm run verify` on the laptop, `npm run push` to GitHub and GitLab, GitHub Actions CI, branch protection on `main`, the Render health check, and a smoke test after each deploy. The golden money cases are frozen once approved. | Each layer catches what the one before can miss. Production changes only through `main`, after CI passes. | Setup time. CI runs only on GitHub, not GitLab. | Accepted |
| D37 | Response-time target: 95% of requests within 500 ms of server time in the Level 1 load test. | Under Lighthouse's 600 ms limit, and leaves room within web.dev's 800 ms "good" time to first byte for a slow phone network. | Doesn't include the real payment company's time. | Accepted |
| D38 | The payment stub picks its outcome from test card numbers (approve, decline, no answer and charged, no answer and not charged, slow approve). It keeps its records in its own file. Demo timings: 10 s payment timeout, 7 s slow approve, the sweep checks attempts pending over 15 s every 5 s. | Every failure path can be shown on video and tested. The slow-approve card plus stopping the database shows the save-failure repair without a "break the code" switch. | Test cards exist only in stub mode, and the outage scene must be recorded locally. | Accepted |

## Assumptions

- **A1:** Today, providers pay practitioner prices (about half of retail) through programs that ship single orders to the patient, such as Emerson Ecologics patient orders and Thorne's practitioner program. Reasons: professional brands sell mainly to licensed practitioners and restrict Amazon sales; the PRD's workflow (order placed for the patient, shipped to the patient, provider pays at checkout) matches these programs; and a provider is unlikely to pay retail when a practitioner price is available. The PRD doesn't say. This only affects the comparison in the write-up.
- **A2:** The hosting budget and host are decided in the architecture step. Resolved: $0 hosting (D34).
- **A3:** The average practitioner-channel order is about $100. No public figure exists. Every order count in ARCHITECTURE.md §2 depends on this.
- **A4:** Each order writes about 10–15 rows (order, lines, events, attempt). This is an estimate for sizing, to be checked against the Level 1 load test.
- **A5:** The original brief stays on the user's machine (`docs/PRD.md` is in `.gitignore`) and isn't published with the repo. These docs quote the parts they rely on, and PROBLEM_SPACE.md restates its requirements.

## Cut, and what's next

| Cut | Why | Next, with more time |
|---|---|---|
| Refunds | Out of scope for the slice (user decision) | Reverse the split. The provider's margin is clawed back. Stripe keeps its processing fee on refunds. |
| Automatic repeat orders (autoship) | The PRD's requirements cover a single order. The slice has Order again (D31) but no schedule. | A recurring schedule the patient agrees to, with a saved card. Each run creates an ordinary order with its own split and Pay flow, possibly with a small discount (Fullscript uses 5%). |
| Patient browsing | Out of scope (PRD) | Let patients browse the provider's store, limited to items the provider allows. |
| Shipping charge | Out of scope (PRD) | Patient pays shipping below a threshold, with free shipping above about $50. Our estimate: a $0-margin order breaks even at about $46 when we pay shipping. |
| Real payments and payouts | Out of scope (PRD) | A Stripe Connect-style setup: we take the charge, then transfer the margin to the provider's connected account. |
| Payee onboarding | Payouts are stubbed | Collect tax details and a bank account, needed only for providers who earn a margin. |
| Practice-level payouts | D13 | Payouts to a practice's account, with each order credited to the recommending provider. |
| Sales tax | Out of scope (PRD) | Calculate and collect tax per state. |
| Stock tracking | D7 | Reduce stock when an order is paid, and stop sales when an item runs out. |
| Pricing-policy review | Regulatory is out of scope | Review brand advertised-price policies (MAP) and FTC rules on comparing to suggested retail prices before showing savings publicly. |
| Measuring % of volume moved | D8 | Ask providers at sign-up how much they spend on third-party sites each month. |
| Card-fee economics | Payments are stubbed | Decide who covers the ~3% card fee, which is larger than the 75 bps. |
| Patient verification | Auth is out of scope (PRD), and a login adds friction (D30) | A one-time code sent by SMS before the order is shown. It needs the patient's phone number from the EHR. |
| Real login | Out of scope (PRD) | The practice's identity provider vouches for the provider, carried in the same JWT cookie (D32) |
| Scaling to Levels 2 and 3 | Built for Level 1 (D18) | Follow the scaling path in ARCHITECTURE.md §2: a read-only database copy, precomputed totals and queued sends, then partitions, splitting data across databases, and a reporting store |
| Standby database | Backups only (D19) | A managed standby that takes over automatically, for the 99.9% target |
| Always-on hosting | $0 hosting is enough for a demo (D34) | Render Starter plus Render Postgres (~$13.30/mo), with the sweep querying only when a payment is pending |
| Editing one draft from two places | The last save wins (D26) | A version number on drafts, and "This draft changed. Reload?" |
| CI on GitLab | GitHub Actions is enough, and the README links to it (D36) | A GitLab CI config, if graders need CI results on GitLab |
| Dark mode | WCAG 2.2 doesn't require it. Light mode reads better for most people, older adults included ([NN/g, 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)). The pay page is short, and patients who need different colours can use a system contrast theme, which the slice supports. | Offer dark mode on the patient page, following the viewer's system setting. It helps people with cataracts or other cloudy vision, who read faster on dark screens because less light scatters in the eye (same NN/g study), and anyone who finds a bright screen uncomfortable, for example at night. Every colour is already a design token, so the work is a second set of values, a contrast check on each pair, and a Playwright run in dark mode. |
